## Minimum owner-facing questions

**1. Exceptional incomplete-liquidation availability (:140–158).** If engineers identify a state with positive opposing inventory and no reachable external conversion depth after removing all own liquidity, may valuation-dependent operations return a typed incomplete-liquidation failure until that condition changes? Recommend explicit failure rather than invented proceeds. This is an exceptional availability policy, not approval to reject normal routes wholesale. Merely having zero *current active* liquidity is insufficient evidence: a swap can traverse a gap to externally funded ticks. The relevant failure is inability to complete the required conversion within the configured pool/domain.

**2. Cost attribution (:146,174–198,240–242).** Confirm the fee table: which charges exist, their bases/recipients/timing, which required user-operation losses reduce that user's credit/payout, and which separately invoked maintenance losses may be borne by holders. Recommend separating simulated costs already in V, actual additional costs, retained fees and externally paid fees. No automatic “one fee to feeTo” default or universal tolerance. Engineering must present supported limits for approval; the owner need not devise them.

**3. Harvest scheduling (:108–116,375).** Should normal idle calls merely observe conversion eligibility, or also perform bounded incidental harvest? Recommend deposits observe, withdrawals realize only what their settled priority requires, and general harvest/zap remain separate. This does not prohibit bounded operation-local close/reopen or overage placement already required by §7A.

**One later approval batch:** sleeve numeric default/bounds/deadband; bootstrap scale/minimum/sink/metadata; native/platform and exact-output scope; target chains/gas margin; engineering-supported maintenance budgets. ## Corrections to peers and to overstatement

* **Incomplete V is a specification tension, not a textual contradiction.** Grok's reading of:154–156 is too absolute: exceptional quote failure can coexist with a useful ordinary domain. Do not default all routes to frozen, and do not import Kimi's partial-value/haircut alternatives as if approved. They change the selected complete-liquidation basis.
* **No premature mint ratification.** Grok recommends approving the growth formula now. Retain it as the candidate expressly labeled at:170–178 until ordering, fees, zero-value states, manipulation and projected-state effects are demonstrated. Owner approval of accounting policy is distinct from engineers validating its formula. The selected V basis is not reopened.
* **Manipulation policy versus proof.** Kimi's `|price move| × opposing share × size − costs` is not a proved bound and may not even have defined compatible units. Temporary external-depth changes can affect whole-liquidation V without a simple spot-price displacement. SEC-08 requires engineers to produce concrete funded attack/cost analyses. If a mitigation introduces gating, a reference source or changed availability, then ask owner approval of that specific proposal. Do not ask for generic acceptance of “bounded extraction” before establishing the bound; per-transaction caps do not bound repeated transactions.
* **Partial execution is not automatically strictly superior.** Keeping owned core liquidity may improve a comparable swap quote, but can also transfer principal/fees within the vault and change remaining liquidation value. Amounts, removals, ticks, limits and fees differ. Kimi's strict-better claim is not a proof of funding or terminal V/share. RV-3 remains an engineering obligation (:190–198), not an owner request to assume concavity.
* **Bootstrap does not imply zero cover.** Target-only initial deposits may remain entirely local: D_target=0, F_target>0. An empty core can coexist with target deployed in a wing. Kimi's automatic early-bootstrap capacity collapse is wrong. No new funded-sleeve eligibility prerequisite is authorized. MiniMax also confuses the lossless *post-placement solution* `T*p/(1+p)` with the current `p*D` comparison when D=0. Existing local target remains real capacity; neither formula destroys it.
* **Do not reopen conversion threshold.** MiniMax's1%-or-floor proposal contradicts actual nonzero opposing-principal eligibility at:112. Fees alone do not qualify. Engineering must implement the precise principal/rounding criterion; harvest scheduling is separate.
* **Retained overage already has a placement path.** :212–214 allows operation-local eligible wing placement, otherwise local retention if blocked/impossible/rounds to zero. It does not require waiting indefinitely for rebalance or forbid all user-path close/reopen. No “overage accumulation policy” vote is needed. “Out-of-range mixed wing” is not a valid principal-composition category for an ordinary position; use actual state and fees separately.
* **Rate provider is not the fee oracle.** V/S is the consumer-facing economic share rate (:285); provider integration and failure semantics are required even without a separate inbound price oracle. `liquidReservePercentageOfVault` is configuration, not a substitute share-rate provider. Target-only route lists and capacity-versus-value semantics must be verified at the consumer ABI, not removed by assumption.
* **Do not cut native/exact-output scope for convenience.** These are unresolved release choices. MiniMax's bisection suggestion is an unapproved numerical method, not an established requirement; first identify supported inverses and consumer obligations. Native underlying currency and user-facing native wrapping are distinct scope dimensions. Grok's ERC20-only/exact-in-only suggestions need approval, not automatic adoption.
