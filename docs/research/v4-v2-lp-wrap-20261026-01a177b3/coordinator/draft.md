## Question

Does the Uniswap V2 Standard Exchange mint vault shares from LP tokens received, and liquidate a holder's LP portion into one token, in a way that eliminates spot-price share manipulation? If so, can an internal wrapper make a Uniswap V4 position play that LP-token role for the Wings vault?

The user's sentence is the hypothesis. It is not a finding.

## Proposed text

Answer: **yes-only-if**. The V2 vault does mint shares from LP tokens received, and it does liquidate a holder's LP portion into one token. That does not eliminate spot-price share manipulation for the product. It removes the spot price from the LP-to-share equation and from the share-to-LP assignment. A V4 wrapper reproduces that narrow property only if its unit is a price-free proportional claim. Naming the current Wings quote an LP token does not.

This is not implementation authority. It does not redefine getRate as NAV. It does not assume an independent price.

### What the V2 code does

The reserve asset is the Uniswap V2 pair. On an exact-in deposit of that pair for vault shares, the executed conversion is BetterMath._convertToShares with floor rounding:

```text
shares = floor(lpReceived * (S + 10^d) / (R_before + 1))
```

The coordinator re-read that function. The numerator offset is `10 ** decimalOffset`. The denominator virtual asset is `+ 1`. The received LP is not added to the denominator. An OutTarget comment that puts `lpReceived` in the denominator is not the executed mint. Grok flagged that comment. The coordinator adopts the converter, not the comment.

`S` is share supply after any fee shares minted in the same call. On the exact-in LP route, `R_before` is the live pair balance before this pull, with a pretransferred inbound subtracted. The zap-in route uses the booked LP reserve and refuses the call unless the live balance already equals that book. Those are two reserve bases. Neither is a spot price.

Exit burns shares and assigns LP with the inverse conversion, floor of `shares * (R + 1) / (S + 10^d)`. If the requested token is not the pair, UniswapV2Service._withdrawSwapDirect burns that LP amount and sells the other token from that burn. It does not pay a fraction of a live whole-book quote, and it does not sell the vault's entire opposing balance.

### Where the user's sentence holds, and where it does not

It holds for the LP-to-share equation and for the share-to-LP assignment. A spot move does not, by itself, change how many vault shares one received LP token mints against a given LP reserve. Each V2 LP token is already an equal claim on the same pair. Counting those tokens does not require a price to combine two inventories.

It does not hold for the vault as a whole.

- The single-token zap calls _swapDeposit on the same pair. That function reads reserves, swaps part of the input, adds both tokens, and returns the LP minted. Spot and the execution price enter how many LP tokens the deposit receives. The share conversion then counts that LP. Avoiding spot in the conversion does not avoid spot in the zap.
- The single-token exit sells the other token from the burned LP through the same pair. Calling that a liquidation of the holder's LP portion is accurate. Calling it the elimination of spot-price share manipulation is not. The share count is no longer the priced step. The trade is.
- Before the depositor conversion, _calcAndMintVaultFee may mint shares to feeTo. The coordinator re-read ConstProdUtils._calculateFeePortionForPosition. It reads the live reserves. The no-fee baseline is `sqrt(initialA * initialB * reserveA / reserveB)`, clamped at zero against the pro-rata claim. Spot can therefore change share supply on an LP deposit, through that fee estimate, as dilution toward feeTo. It does not set lpReceived or R_before. Whether that mint is economically a price channel, or only a fee-growth estimate, is a preserved disagreement. It was not executed as a profit trace. It is enough to reject the sentence "spot is not used to mint shares" if that sentence is applied to the whole call rather than to the LP-to-share equation.

### What this does to the prior mechanisms

The prior swap-capture report is cited, not re-executed. Its closed finding remains: a deposit swap plus an exit swap does not eliminate quote-rule or executed-price extraction while the mint prices inventory the swap did not trade. This pattern is a different mint. It is not a rehabilitation of that swap pair.

- Quote manipulation, as `floor(S * D / V)` against a live whole-book quote, does not run on the V2 LP-to-share leg and does not run on a wrapper that counts homogeneous units. It remains on current Wings production, where Accounting.issue uses `floor(supply * credit / value)` and value comes from the quote. It returns if a wrapper prices the position, the idle sleeve, the other wings, or the fees into one denominator.
- Executed-price allocation of old inventory, in the prior report's form, does not run on the LP-to-share equation. The credit unit and the reserve unit are both LP. The zap still produces that unit by trading. Whether that production is "the prior mechanism relocated" or "entrant-borne execution that does not reprice the old book" is a preserved disagreement. Both descriptions agree that the zap is not the safe leg, and that a deposit swap is not a fix by itself. A preparatory swap can still trade against liquidity the vault owns and change what the remaining LP contains. Astra's zero-fee illustration of an exit sale changing remaining pair composition is an algebraic limit, not an executed profit witness. The prior self-fill sign split is not resolved here.
- Vault self-fill does not run on a pure LP deposit or a pure LP withdrawal, because those do not swap. It remains available on the zap and on the exit swap whenever the vault's liquidity is in the pool that fills them. V2 narrows the exit sale to the burned LP's other token. It does not remove the fill. A V4 range the vault owns can be the whole fill, not a pro-rata slice of an external pair. No sign or profit is assigned to that case.

### What a wrapper must emit

A V4 position is not a V2 LP token. Inventory.principal clamps the current square-root price into the range and returns amount0 and amount1. Those amounts change when the price moves, with no trade. composition can be one-sided. earnedFee is separate and is not converted principal. The Wings book is Core, Upper, and Lower liquidity at the PoolManager, plus idle balances. There is no external fungible LP token to deposit the way a caller deposits a V2 pair token.

Two shapes can reproduce the safe leg. They are not the same design, and this draft does not pick one as the only sufficient shape.

1. One homogeneous receipt per `(pool, range)`. The receipt is minted when liquidity is added to that one range, one unit per equal liquidity claim, and burned when that liquidity is removed. Ranges are not mixed inside one unit. Idle balances, uncollected fees, and the other slots stay outside the unit. The outer mint counts units received against units held, with the same converter shape as V2. This is the shape Grok and Kimi require. MiniMax requires the same price-free unit and treats a single unit across a core-plus-wings book as a drift into a mark.
2. A componentwise basket. The backing vector is idle0, idle1, and liquidity per identified range, with old fees already attributed or held in a separate entitlement. A new unit is minted only for a contribution that funds every backed component in its own units, unmatched input refunded in kind. Astra's acceptance identity is `U / (U + w) * (B + C) = B` before favorable rounding. That is a per-component join. It is not a sum of unlike amounts and not a price-weighted NAV. The prior report accepted that identity only at acceptance time, not as post-sale immunity.

What fails is a wrapper whose unit is principal, Quote.quote, a sum of liquidity across ranges, or the current Wings issue calculation with a new name. Equal numerical liquidity in two ranges is not the same claim. A one-sided wing has no other token for the V2 withdraw-and-swap to sell. If a single target token cannot fund every required component, the wrapper cannot manufacture the missing leg. The same pool may be the only market.

If the outer vault owns the entire internal wrapper and neither layer has fees or offsets, the outer conversion adds no economic protection beyond correct inner accounting. The receipt does not have to be a transferable ERC-20. It does have to have enforceable mint, burn, and backing.

### Equations, if a sufficient shape is met

Receipt deposit, new units excluded from the denominator:

```text
shares = floor(uReceived * (S + 10^d) / (U_before + 1))
```

No square-root price. No Accounting.issue against a liquidation quote. No same-pool TWAP. The TWAP note in the prior run remains a poke record, not an independent price, and this design does not use it as a mint input.

Single-token entry is not the safe leg. A swap through the pool, then an add of liquidity, produces uReceived. The conversion above then counts that receipt. The swap is where spot and execution enter.

Exit:

```text
uOut = floor(shares * (U + 1) / (S + 10^d))
remove that slice of the receipt's backing
sell only the non-target token from that removal
pay the target from the removal plus the sale proceeds
```

Removal uses the current square-root price to split that slice into tokens. That is composition at removal, the same kind of fact as a V2 burn returning pro-rata reserves. It is not the share count. The sale must not pay entitlement(shares, quote, supply), and it must not sell the vault's remaining opposing inventory to fund this redeemer. If the sale cannot complete, the contract reverts or returns residual in kind. It does not treat unfilled opposing as target, and it does not borrow target from the incumbent sleeve to satisfy a quote.

If a later design has getRate, it is the normalized preview of that one-whole-share removal and sale. It is not liquidateAll(book) / supply, and it is not an issuance NAV. Current Wings getRate is the whole-book quote over supply. This draft does not change it and does not copy it into the wrapper.

A hook that holds the vault values the reserve as raw balance, or as balance times an external rate provider. It does not derive that rate from this Standard Exchange, from the V2 withdraw-swap preview, or from the same-pool TWAP.

### What the adaptation does not provide

- Elimination of spot-price exposure on single-token entry or exit.
- An independent price.
- A fungible token that the Wings position already is.
- One receipt that holds Core, Upper, Lower, idle balances, and uncollected fees without either a price or an in-kind per-component join.
- Fee accrual inside a raw liquidity unit. V2 LP captures fee growth in the pair. V4 fees sit outside principal. A fee path that marks uncollected fees at the current price reintroduces a spot-dependent quantity. A fee path that reads reserves, as V2's fee-share mint does, is the qualification above, not a new safe leg.
- Removal of composition risk. A square-root move changes what one liquidity unit contains, including a move from mixed to one-sided, without changing the share count.
- A no-profitable-round-trip theorem, or preservation of incumbent token principal after the exit sale.
- The settled-target fee-vault design from the other report. That design does not put the depositor's capital in the pool. This question asks whether the position can play the LP role. Those are different objects.
- Implementation authority.
