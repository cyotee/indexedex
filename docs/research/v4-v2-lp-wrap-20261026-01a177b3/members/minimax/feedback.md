## Concerns

**C1. Shape 1 and the single-`U` equation are not reconciled, and this is the load-bearing gap.** The draft records two sufficient wrapper shapes. Shape 1 is "one homogeneous receipt per `(pool, range)`", with the outer mint counting "units received against units held" and the outer equation `shares = floor(uReceived * (S + 10^d) / (U_before + 1))`. The draft also states that what fails is "a sum of liquidity across ranges", because equal numerical liquidity in two ranges is not the same claim. For the Wings book, which is Core, Upper and Lower plus idle balances, those two statements cannot both hold: if the outer vault holds receipts in three ranges and counts them against one `U`, the denominator is a sum of liquidity across ranges, which the draft rejects; if each range gets its own denominator, the outer vault has one share supply `S` and three reserve bases, and the draft gives no rule for combining them. The draft needs to state which of these it means: an outer book restricted to a single range, per-range sub-ledgers with a componentwise join, or a range-homogeneous unit whose definition survives range changes and closures. As written, the sufficiency claim for Shape 1 is not checkable, because the equation and the failure rule are in direct tension. This is a synthesis gap rather than a factual error: my own response flagged that a single unit across a core-plus-wings book drifts into a mark, and the draft records that as a preserved disagreement, but it then offers Shape 1 as sufficient without resolving how the outer reserve is denominated.

**C2. The exit sequence omits the fee entitlement of the burned slice.** The draft puts fees deliberately outside the unit ("uncollected fees and the other slots stay outside the unit", or "held in a separate entitlement" under Shape 2), and the draft's own "does not provide" list confirms that "fee accrual inside a raw liquidity unit" is not provided. But the exit is written as: remove that slice of the receipt's backing, sell only the non-target token from that removal, pay the target from the removal plus the sale proceeds. If fees sit outside the receipt, the redeemer's slice of uncollected fees has to be realised and paid at the same time, or the exit silently pays the redeemer principal only while the fee entitlement stays in the vault and dilutes the remaining holders. The draft should either add the fee-realisation step to the exit sequence or state that fees are collected into the receipt before mint and are therefore inside `U`. Either is coherent; leaving it unspecified makes the exit sequence incomplete, and the fee path is exactly where the V2 pattern differs most sharply, since a V2 LP token captures fee growth in the pair while a V4 position does not.

**C3. The executed-price-allocation framing risks conflating two different things.** The draft says the prior mechanism "does not run on the LP-to-share equation" because "the credit unit and the reserve unit are both LP", and records as a preserved disagreement whether the zap's quantity effect is "the prior mechanism relocated" or "entrant-borne execution that does not reprice the old book". The structural point that makes the second reading the right one is not stated: in the prior report's form, the loss came from mixing units, with a credit in one unit booked against a reserve priced in another, so the old book was mispriced at acceptance. In the V2 pattern the old book is never repriced at acceptance, because the minted claim and the reserve are the same unit and the entrant's acquisition is a trade the entrant makes. What remains is a different and ordinary risk, namely that the entrant's swap moves the pool price and the vault's LP, as an LP in the same pool, absorbs the impact. That is pool-level exposure to a third party's trade, not an accounting allocation against old inventory, and the distinction is the part of the V2 pattern most worth copying. The draft's own line "a preparatory swap can still trade against liquidity the vault owns and change what the remaining LP contains" is right, but it is currently filed under a preserved disagreement where it belongs under the structural difference between the two designs. Left as is, a reader could take the V2 zap to be dangerous in the prior report's sense.

**C4. The acceptance identity is restated with a notation change that should be flagged.** The draft gives Astra's acceptance identity as `U / (U + w) * (B + C) = B`. The prior report states it as `S / (S + m) * (B + C) = B`, where the ownership fraction is over shares. The two are algebraically equivalent only under proportional issuance, where `m/S = w/U`, so `S/(S+m) = U/(U+w)`. The draft should say so in one clause. Without it, the substitution reads as though the incumbent's ownership fraction were taken over units rather than over shares, which is not the claim being made.

**C5. One executed fact sits outside the stated evidence scope.** The draft's own Limits and Evidence gaps scope the coordinator's re-read to `BetterMath._convertToShares` and `ConstProdUtils._calculateFeePortionForPosition`. But the "What the V2 code does" section asserts, as coordinator text, that "the zap-in route uses the booked LP reserve and refuses the call unless the live balance already equals that book", and that on the exact-in LP route `R_before` is the live pair balance before the pull with a pretransferred inbound subtracted. Both assertions are about `UniswapV2StandardExchangeInTarget`, which is outside that re-read scope. I checked them directly in the source: the zap-in branch converts with `BetterMath._convertToSharesDown(lpReceived, vault.vaultLpReserve, vault.vaultTotalShares, decimalOffset)` against the pre-zap booked reserve, and reverts when `indexSource.pool.balanceOf(address(this)) != vault.vaultLpReserve`; the exact-in LP branch sets `reserveBefore` to the live balance, subtracting the inbound when `pretransferred`. The claims are correct. The attribution is what needs tightening: they should be marked as adopted from the members who opened the file rather than as coordinator-verified, so the evidence chain matches the stated scope. I note the draft already discloses the analogous gap for the V4 files in its coverage table, so this is a consistency point, not a discovery.

**C6. The adaptation provides one thing the draft does not list.** The draft's "What the adaptation does not provide" is thorough and correct, and its note that "the receipt does not have to be a transferable ERC-20" points at a real advantage without drawing the consequence. A V2 LP token is fungible and transferable, so a holder can withdraw LP from the vault and then burn or sell it into the pool at a time of their own choosing, externalising that price impact onto the remaining LPs, including the vault's own position. The Wings position is not fungible and its holder cannot extract it; a non-transferable receipt keeps it that way. So on this channel the wrapper is strictly better than the pattern it copies, and the draft should say so, together with the converse: a transferable receipt reopens a channel the current Wings design does not have. This is a genuine "what the adaptation provides" item that the draft currently lists only in the negative.

**C7. Minor precision, no objection to the content.** The draft says the V2 exit "does not sell the vault's entire opposing balance", which is right and is a real contrast with the Wings `_swapDeficit` path. It would help to add one clause that the contrast is about quantity, not about direction: narrowing the sale reduces the magnitude of the price impact roughly in proportion to the size of the sale, but the impact is still created by the vault's own liquidity, so the channel is attenuated rather than closed. The draft's sentence "It does not remove the fill" already carries this; the addition would just prevent an over-reading of the narrowing.
