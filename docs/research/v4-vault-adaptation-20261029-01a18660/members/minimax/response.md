# MiniMax original — which V4 fee vault should IndexedEx adapt?

Access date for every public source: 2026-10-29. Local files were read on 2026-10-29 and may change; I cite the functions/lines I actually read.

## 0. Recommendation, concretely

**Do not adopt any of the three as a codebase. Adopt Weír's *shape* as the foundation — but re-host it inside the local `UniswapV4PairTokenStandardExchangeVault` family, which is already Weir-shaped — and take lpTOKEN's zap invariants and The Pool's issuance/rate discipline as donors.**

- **Preferred foundation: Weír's vault shape.** The decisive reason is **topology, not taste**: the vault must mint/redeem and deliver a single token *inside our own hook's swap callback*, i.e. inside an already-unlocked PoolManager. Only a vault that owns its position **directly** on the PoolManager can legally act in that context (canonical `modifyLiquidity`/`swap` are `onlyWhenUnlocked`). Weír (direct position, salt-0, single contract, native `zap`/`zapTo`) qualifies; lpTOKEN's single-token path lives in a separate router that opens its own `poolManager.unlock` (verified in P3 source) and The Pool's position layer runs through the PositionManager (admitted P8) — both are structurally unavailable inside our callback.
- **Runner-up as a donor, not as a base: The Pool** — for its *single-fixed-asset* rate shape and its rule that entry shares are priced from the **post-conversion NAV delta**, which is the same principle as local `S1-ACC-07`.
- **Best periphery/invariant donor: lpTOKEN** — for the full-input-consumption, whole-leg conversion and revert-on-partial-fill discipline that local `S1-X12` demands and that Weír does not provide.
- **The user's Weír preference is directionally right and I do not defer to it:** Weír's *issuance* rule, *fee*, *dead shares* and *residual-tolerant exit* are all things the local PRD has already rejected (`S1-X15`, `S1-X04`, `S1-X02`, `S1-X12`). Weír is the closest **shape**, not the closest **code**.
- **Disposal:** ALPS (conditional cash exit; its V4 adapter returns a zero TWAP and *skips* the V4 auto-unwind — verified P11), USDa (dual-token fallback bypassing `minOut` — the opposite of `S1-X12`), Unoxi (asynchronous rounds, confidential wrapper, operator-posted `PinnedReference`), ZapVault (per-user custody, no share token, no min-out, residual retained in vault custody) are **not** candidates. Keep their *lessons*, discard their products.

## 2. "We just need to add the ERC20" — not sufficient, and in this repo the framing is wrong

In the local architecture the vault **is** the share token: `UniswapV4PairTokenStandardExchangeVaultSYQueryTarget.yieldToken()` returns `address(this)` ("This diamond is the sole public share token"), and `S1-ARCH-02` fixes the shape: *"The diamond is the ERC-20 and the owner used in the PoolManager position key"*, shares 18 decimals, one position over the full usable tick range at salt `bytes32(0)`, `rateAsset` = one of the two ERC-20 faces. So "add ERC20" means *re-express upstream share accounting inside the diamond's `ERC20Repo` facets* — not wrap an upstream vault in a token. Concretely, an ERC20 layer alone is insufficient because:

1. **Share unit.** Weír's shares are liquidity-ratio units (`shares = liq * totalShares / liquidity`); an18-decimal token needs a fixed-point mapping, and the rate math must distinguish one whole share from raw units.
2. **Dead shares.** Weír's first deposit permanently mints `MIN_SHARES = 1_000` with no holder and `totalShares = MIN_SHARES` ("never redeemable"). `S1-X02` forbids a permanent sink; `S1-X10` requires a zero-supply reset/terminal withdrawal. Weir's mechanism must be replaced by a virtual-offset or zero-supply design.
3. **Entrant conversion-cost attribution.** Weír's `_balance` swap happens **inside the pool the vault itself provides liquidity to**, so the swap's price impact is borne by the vault's remaining position and by other LPs, not by the entrant. `S1-ACC-07` requires the opposite: freeze the incumbent snapshot **after** the caller's swap, fund the addition from caller inventory only, and mint `qCredit = min_i floor(S * C_i / R_i)` with `R_i = N_i(P*,L) + B_i`. Weír's issuance rule is exactly the gross-deployment rule `S1-X15` rejects; The Pool's post-conversion NAV-delta rule is the nearest upstream analogue.
4. **Idle/dust.** Weír tracks `idle0`/`idle1` as dust re-added at the next compound — a good model that maps onto the local booked-idle `B_i`; but the ownership policy (pro-rata to shares vs a reserve) is undecided and changes issuance fairness and the rate.
5. **Partial-withdrawal fee settlement.** Canonical v4 `modifyLiquidity` credits `callerDelta = principal + fees`, so a partial removal settles **all** accrued fees to the caller. Weír's mitigation is ordering (compound → then remove); the local law is stronger and explicit: `S1-X16` requires a final non-recursive fee checkpoint before entry/exit allocation, and `S1-EXIT-01`'s transition table says *"Burned shares cannot receive later fee growth of remaining liquidity"* and *"Fees earned by remaining position liquidity belong to remaining holders."*
6. **Protocol fee.** `S1-X04`: no stage-one entry/exit/performance/protocol fee. Weír's `perfFeeBps` (default 5 %, cap 10 %) must not be ported; lpTOKEN's 30 bps share fee likewise.
7. **Transferable-share hazards that a non-transferable ledger did not have.** Donation/inflation (`S1-X09` explicit donation adoption; booked inventory isolated from unbooked pretransfers via `LocalCreditLib`), flash-share/mint-and-donate ordering, and the fact that a **downstream pool now holds a share whose redemption is exercised by a third party** — the redemption must not be priceable in a way that lets the holder extract more than pro-rata.

## 3. Same-PoolManager already-unlocked execution

**What the canonical manager actually permits (v4-core `PoolManager.sol`, read this run).**
- `unlock(bytes)` reverts `AlreadyUnlocked` if `Lock.isUnlocked()` — **a nested unlock is impossible**. So Weír-style `deposit`/`withdraw` (which call `poolManager.unlock(...)`) and any PositionManager action cannot run inside our hook's callback.
- `modifyLiquidity` and `swap` are `onlyWhenUnlocked` + `noDelegateCall` — they **are** callable inside an existing unlock, without nesting. This is the door.
- `unlock` ends with `if (NonzeroDeltaCount.read() != 0) revert CurrencyNotSettled()`. Deltas are **per (currency, caller)**, but the *unsettled* counter is **global per currency**: any unresolved delta from any address fails the whole transaction. So a vault that leaves a residual inside the callback does not merely mis-pay — it kills the user's swap.
- `IPoolManager` NatSpec: `callerDelta` = principal + fee + hook deltas; `feesAccrued` is "for informational purposes" and "can be artificially inflated by a malicious actor".
- PositionManager: its action path is an unlock-callback path (`msgSender()` returns `_getLocker()`, and its own NatSpec says it may be used "ONLY if the call is being made from within the unlockCallback"). Its `poolManager.unlock` call site lives in `BaseActionsRouter._executeActions`, which I did **not** read — treat "PositionManager unusable inside an unlocked manager" as strong inference pending that one file.

**Minimal authorized in-callback settlement design (prose).** The vault keeps two entry surfaces, not one:
(a) the ordinary user surface, which opens its own unlock and is unchanged; and
(b) an explicitly *hook-context* surface, callable **only** by our registered hook, which **never calls `unlock`** and instead performs its operations inside the caller's already-open context: `poolManager.modifyLiquidity` for the vault's own position (add/remove/poke), `poolManager.swap` for the conversion, `poolManager.sync`+`settle` to pay, `poolManager.take` to receive. The authorization must be the local pattern, not a bare unlock check: caller == registered hook **and** the hook is the hook of the pool being swapped **and** the vault's own `busy`/operation state is set **and** the callback payload hash matches (local `S1-X11`: *"Authenticate the PoolManager plus an active operation and its expected callback payload"*), **and** the manager's transient unlocked flag is set as a *corroborating* condition only. A bare `isUnlocked()` bypass is not acceptable: any third party can open an unlock on the manager and then call the vault, so the unlocked flag proves context, not authority.

**Exact one-token postcondition inside the callback.** Because the callback's deltas must net to zero, the vault cannot "take" a leftover unwanted token without silently delivering two tokens. The strict rule (local `S1-X12`: *"A single-token exit must consume its entire unwanted-token allocation or revert atomically. Paired exit remains the fallback."*) therefore requires: convert the **entire** unwanted leg with exact-input; assert the spent input equals the requested input and the unwanted leg's output is zero; otherwise revert the whole swap. lpTOKEN's router is the reference implementation of that invariant (`InexactSwapInput` / `InexactSwapOutput` / full-spend checks, whole-leg conversion). Weír's behaviour — settling both deltas and paying the residual to the recipient — is a **two-token outcome**, not a one-token outcome, and must not be carried over. `S1-EXIT-01` reinforces this: the collection-only fallback *"must satisfy the same final sale/full-consumption constraints when single-token output is requested"*, and *"an unavailable final single-token sale"* is explicitly **not** a fallback trigger — the caller may choose the **paired route explicitly** (not silently). That matches the user's requirement: single-token success delivers exactly one token; otherwise the transaction reverts.

**Other topology facts the design must respect.**
- *Direct vault-owner liquidity* is the enabler: the vault must be the position owner (`(diamond, tickLower, tickUpper, bytes32(0))`, `S1-ARCH-02`), so its own `modifyLiquidity` calls need no NFT and no second unlock.
- *Nested hook recursion*: keep the **backing pool hookless** (`S1-X06` already requires a hookless pool) so the vault's internal conversion never re-enters a hook. If the owner later adds a TWAP hook to the backing pool, the vault's conversion swap re-enters that hook and needs an explicit recursion guard; ALPS's `UniV4Adapter` is the cautionary precedent (its `swapExactIn` routes through a v4 router and its `getTwapSqrtPriceX96` returns 0 on purpose).
- *Transient accounting / read consistency*: transient state (`Lock`, `NonzeroDeltaCount`) is visible only inside the unlocked window. A **rate provider called by Balancer is in a different call context** and cannot see transient state, so the rate must be computed from persistent state (position liquidity, fee growth, booked idle, supply). The Wings family's `sleeveState()` exposing `manager.isUnlocked()` is the local precedent for transient-aware views; the new rate target must not depend on it.
- *A different PoolManager is a topology condition, not a universal fix.* Putting the backing pool on a second manager M2 would let the vault open `M2.unlock()` inside our M1 callback (independent locks), but it doubles manager state, breaks the local single-`config.poolManager` binding, and adds a second failure domain. Recommendation: **same manager, direct position, no second manager.**
- *Local guards already in place*: `UniswapV4PairTokenStandardExchangeVaultExecution._start` reverts `IVault.ManagerAlreadyUnlocked()` when the manager is unlocked, requires `Repo._requireIdle`, and consults the registry's issuance-disable switch; `UniswapV4UpperLowerWingsStandardExchangeVaultCommon.execute` binds an expected callback payload hash (`state.callbackHash`), enforces operation and mutation budgets (≤8 ops, ≤16 mutations), re-checks `callbackHash == 0` after `unlock`, and reverts `ManagerAlreadyUnlocked`. These are the exact primitives the hook-context path should reuse rather than weaken.

## 4. Three concepts that must stay separate, and the verdict on a "one-share exit rate"

| Concept | Where it lives locally | What it may be used for |
|---|---|---|
| Proportional ownership / share issuance | `S1-ACC-07` (attributable entry credit), `ERC20Repo` supply | minting/burning; **never** a rate |
| Accounting / SY value | `exchangeRate()` = `Math.accountingRate(sqrtPriceX96, amount0, amount1, supply, isToken0)` — NatSpec: *"Spot-marked fee-inclusive basket accounting, not an exit quote or issuance input"* (`S1-P06`) | display, discovery, accounting |
| Executable withdrawal-and-sale quote | amount-specific; `S1-QUOTE-01` requires execution-equivalent projection (≤64 swap steps, ≤12 initialized-tick crossings, ≤16 bitmap words) with the same model as execution | user-facing guarantee (min-out) |

**Verdict: a normalized one-share executable quote is *not* fit as a linear downstream invariant multiplier.** Reasons, all local: (i) it is size-dependent — the marginal price of selling N shares is not N times the price of selling one share, so a one-share rate cannot price arbitrary pool sizes; (ii) `S1-QUOTE-01` explicitly forbids using *"a spot quote, whole-book liquidation divided by supply, or an off-chain quoter"* as a substitute for an on-chain view, and the Wings `getRate()` is precisely whole-portfolio liquidation ÷ supply — a **normalized portfolio rate, not a one-share exit** (it must not be relabelled as one); (iii) Balancer's `IRateProvider` is a legacy 18-decimal interface with **no rounding direction and no error channel** ("the caller might require a certain rounding direction… great care must be taken"), so a rate whose denominator is a live, manipulable, same-pool quote is the worst possible input to an invariant; (iv) reflexivity: the rate is read by the pool that trades the share, while the vault's exit sells into the backing pool — the rate and the exit move each other.

**Recommendation (one concrete rate design, and the alternative labelled as such).** Publish the downstream multiplier as the vault's **standard-size marginal redemption rate** in the related underlying, 18-decimal, refreshed on a bounded cadence with an explicit staleness rule, and bound redemption to that standard size (larger redemptions are rate-limited/queued — the classic rate-limited-redemption pattern), so the downstream pool cannot outbid the vault's own payout and no arbitrage gap opens between pool price and redemption price. Keep the stage-one SY accounting rate as a *separate, explicitly labelled* surface (`S1-P06`), and expose the executable quote as an amount-specific preview with min-out. *If the owner instead wants the accounting rate (or the Wings-style normalized portfolio rate) as the Balancer multiplier, that is a **new product choice** with a stated safety caveat — a rate that is not the vault's realizable payout rate can be arbitraged against the vault, bounded only by the redemption limit — not an accepted replacement of the exit quote, and it must be recorded as such.*

## 5. The no-independent-market premise

Under the premise that the target may have liquidity **only in the backing pool**, and without assuming Chainlink, a second deep pool, a peg or an independent market: the only available reference for valuing the vault's position in target tokens is the backing pool's own price — and v4 has **no built-in oracle** (ALPS's adapter says so explicitly and returns 0 rather than spot: *"V4 PoolManager has no built-in oracle (oracles must be hooks) … returning spot here would silently let the vault swap at attacker-set prices behind a slippage floor that was itself derived from the manipulated spot — strictly worse than skipping"*, verified P11). Consequences for this product: a **TWAP in the backing pool** is the only non-spot reference, and it is **not independent** of the pool the vault must sell into — a manipulator moves spot immediately and the TWAP with a lag; it buys time, not safety. Therefore: (i) the rate must be explicitly documented as TWAP-anchored-or-spot with a bounded staleness window and a "last valid" fallback, never as a safe or fair price; (ii) exits must be bounded relative to available backing depth (owner-approved limits, with the *atomicity* of the single-token route preserved — the brief's limit allowance is not permission to soften the postcondition); (iii) the rate must not be derived from a spot quote that the same transaction can move; (iv) if the owner wants a manipulation-resistant reference, adding a TWAP-providing hook to the backing pool is the only route — and that **conflicts with `S1-X06`'s hookless-pool requirement**, so it is an explicit owner decision to escalate, not a silent change; (v) the honest precedent is ALPS's: when no manipulation-resistant reference exists, **skip the conversion** rather than quote a manipulable price. Our product's hard requirement (synchronous single-token exit inside a swap) sits in direct tension with that precedent, and the resolution is *bounded size + documented reference weakness*, not a safety claim.

## 6. Minimal required adaptations (bounded; no implementation plan)

1. **Share re-expression** — replace the upstream liquidity-ratio ledger with 18-decimal `ERC20Repo` supply on the local `S1-ACC-07` credit equation; no permanent sink (`S1-X02`); zero-supply reset/terminal withdrawal (`S1-X10`).
2. **Single-token entry/exit inside the vault** — a zap branch on the vault's operation kind (the local family already has `OperationKind.DepositZap`), no separate router, so it can run in the hook context.
3. **Strict one-token postcondition** — lpTOKEN's invariants: whole-leg conversion, exact-input full-spend assertion, revert on partial fill (`S1-X12`); no residual paid to the recipient.
4. **Attributable entry credit and post-swap incumbent snapshot** — The Pool's issuance principle, expressed in the local `S1-ACC-07` equation; caller-only inventory; no gross deployment from idle (`S1-X15`).
5. **Fee checkpoint discipline** — non-recursive final fee checkpoint before entry/exit allocation (`S1-X16`), harvest-before-peel, and `callerDelta`-based fee accounting (never `feesAccrued`, which is donation-inflatable).
6. **No protocol fee, no dead shares, no 10 bps retention** (`S1-X04`, `S1-X03`, `S1-X02`).
7. **Authorized hook-context path** — reuse `S1-X11` + the Wings `execute` pattern (caller + expected payload hash + operation/mutation budgets + transient unlocked corroboration); never an unguarded `isUnlocked()` bypass; never a second PoolManager.
8. **Two rate surfaces, explicitly separated** — SY accounting rate (existing) and a Balancer `IRateProvider` target (the Wings family already implements Crane's `IRateProvider` + `IStandardExchangeRateQuote`, so no new interface is needed), each with a stated basis, staleness rule and rounding direction.
9. **Execution-equivalent previews with bounded budgets** (`S1-QUOTE-01`, `S1-GAS-01`, `S1-P07` 12 M-gas ceiling): previews must project the same operation, including the maintenance-mode choice and the final single-token sale against post-removal liquidity — not a spot quote, not whole-book liquidation ÷ supply.
10. **Maintenance fallback stays local** — `S1-EXIT-01`'s `CollectOnly` mode is selected by deterministic preflight; "already-unlocked manager" is explicitly **not** a fallback trigger, so the hook path must be a designed mode, not an exception.
11. **Native currency** retained (`S1-P02`), with native sync reset immediately before value settlement (`S1-X18`).

## 7. Brief disposal of the rest

- **ALPS** — not a base: multi-pool basket, agent-managed, and its V4 cash exit is structurally unavailable (adapter TWAP = 0 ⇒ auto-unwind skipped; verified P11). *Keep:* the honesty precedent of refusing to quote rather than quoting a manipulable price.
- **USDa** — best *accounting discipline* (donation-proof NAV, per-holder fee-surplus retention, explicit dual-token fallback) but the fallback **bypasses the caller's `minOut`** — the opposite of `S1-X12`; testnet only; peg-clamp assumes a stable pair.
- **Unoxi** — asynchronous two-sided rounds, confidential wrapper, `PinnedReference` (operator-posted price): incompatible with a synchronous single-token redemption, and the reference is not a market oracle.
- **ZapVault** — per-user custody, no share token, no min-out or deadline, residual retained in vault custody on a partial fill: incompatible with pooled shares and an exact postcondition.

## 8. Genuinely undecided — owner decisions (not for me to close)

1. **Which rate is the Balancer multiplier.** My recommendation: standard-size marginal redemption rate with bounded redemption; the accounting rate as multiplier is a *new product choice* with an explicit arbitrage caveat. Needs the owner's call, with the security view recorded.
2. **Backing-pool reference.** Add a TWAP-providing hook to the backing pool (breaks `S1-X06` hookless requirement) or accept spot-only with documented weakness. Escalate; do not silently pick.
3. **Redemption bounds and rate refresh cadence** (and the rounding direction the Balancer interface cannot express).
4. **Idle/dust ownership** — pro-rata to shares (Weír) vs a reserve; changes issuance fairness and the rate.
5. **Whether the hook-context path may run a full compound** (gas, `S1-P07` 12 M ceiling) or must use the `CollectOnly` fallback (staleness of the rate).
6. **Whether the vault ever takes a manager/performance fee** — recommendation: no, per `S1-X04`; any upstream fee mechanism (Weír's `perfFeeBps`, lpTOKEN's 30 bps, The Pool's ≤20 %) is excluded.
7. **Whether the backing pool stays hookless** (recommend yes, for recursion and for `S1-X06`), i.e. our hook lives only on the share-consuming pool.
