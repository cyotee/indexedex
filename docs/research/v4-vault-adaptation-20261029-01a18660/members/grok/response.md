Why adding ERC20 is not sufficient. Observed in the Sourcify-matched `src/WeirVaults.sol` on 2026-10-29: `deposit`, `withdraw`, and `compound` each call `poolManager.unlock`. `_withdraw` removes a pro-rata liquidity slice, adds pro-rata idle, optionally swaps the unwanted positive net, then `_settle`s both currencies to the recipient and returns both positive amounts. `withdraw` then checks only `out0 < min0 || out1 < min1`. A zero minimum on the unwanted leg does not require its absence. `MIN_SHARES` is 1,000, assigned to `totalShares` and never minted to the recipient. Later issuance is `liquidity minted * totalShares / existing liquidity`. The default performance fee is 500 bps, owner-settable under a 10% cap. Pause blocks deposit, not the withdraw entry. None of that is fixed by wrapping the ledger in ERC20.

Same-manager settlement. Canonical `PoolManager.unlock` reverts `AlreadyUnlocked` if the manager is already unlocked. `modifyLiquidity`, `swap`, `settle`, `take`, and `sync` are the unlocked operations. Deltas are accounted to `msg.sender`, not to the original unlocker. `NonzeroDeltaCount` is global and must be zero when the outer `unlock` returns. `sync` writes one global synced currency.

So a hook callback on the share-consuming pool cannot call vanilla Weir, lpTOKEN, or The Pool on the same PoolManager. The first nested `unlock` reverts. A second PoolManager would let those calls unlock. That is a deployment condition, not the design. Do not add an unguarded `isUnlocked` bypass that still calls `unlock`, and do not skip the guard and then touch the manager without a bound caller and a zero-delta return.

The local code already refuses this topology. `UniswapV4PairTokenStandardExchangeVaultExecution._start` and `_action` revert `ManagerAlreadyUnlocked` and `_action` then calls `unlock`. Wings `execute` does the same. The stage-one PRD says the same at the already-unlocked paragraph and at the callback rule: check `isUnlocked` before a new unlock, revert, and do not inherit a local-sleeve fallback.

Minimal authorized entry, in prose only:

Keep every public deposit, redeem, and compound on the current guard. Add a separate entry callable only by the registered share-pool hook, and only while the manager is already unlocked. It must not call `unlock`. It calls `modifyLiquidity` and `swap` on the hookless backing pool. Those deltas belong to the vault. Before it returns, the vault settles or takes only its own two currency deltas back to zero. It must not settle, take, or clear the hook's or the router's deltas. It must not leave `sync` aimed at a currency the outer locker did not sync; save and restore that global slot, or avoid a nested sync. Native value inside someone else's unlock is the sharp case.

The backing pool stays hookless and is not the share pool. `modifyLiquidity` and `swap` call the pool's hook while the manager is unlocked. If that hook is this hook, the call recurses. Stage one already requires a hookless backing pool.

Authorization is the hook address, the backing pool key, and the operation, held behind the existing busy flag. It is not "if unlocked, proceed." The public callback remains payload-bound to an unlock this vault started. The hook entry is an external call from the hook, not that callback.

A successful hook swap pays one token or reverts. Idle dust may remain inside the shareholder basket. It may not be paid to the swapper and it may not be left as the swapper's residual custody. Price impact and depth may revert the swap. That is the accepted failure. Exact output stays unsupported.

Ownership, issuance, and the three rates. Keep these apart.

Issuance is how many shares a deposit mints. Use stage one's rule: caller-only entry inventory, and mint against attributable reserve growth at the post-swap incumbent snapshot. Do not use Weir's liquidity-ratio mint, which includes idle dust and a permanently locked share residual. Do not use The Pool's scalar NAV as the share formula. The Pool's net-added idea is a useful warning that the entrant must bear the zap cost. The two-currency basket should not be collapsed into ERC4626 `asset()` to get that warning.

Accounting rate is what stage one already publishes. `exchangeRate()` loads spot position amounts and calls `accountingRate`, which converts one leg at spot, floors, then returns `floor(value * 1e18 / supply)` in raw rate-asset units per 1e18 raw share units. The PRD's own example: value of 1e6 raw units and supply of 1e18 gives rate 1e6. One whole share is then 1e6 raw units of a six-decimal asset, not 1e18. The NatSpec and S1-P06 say this is not an exit quote and not an issuance input. It can fall when price moves. Fee earning does not make it monotonic.

Wings `getRate` is a different function. It divides `Quote.quote(...).value` by supply and scales by 1e18. That is a whole-portfolio liquidation figure per raw share, not a simulated exit of one whole share. Do not copy it onto this vault and call it the exit rate.

The executable quote is the amount-specific remove-and-sell preview. It is not linear in size. A quote for one whole share, however normalized, does not promise that N shares return N times that amount after the unwanted leg is sold in the only pool.

Balancer's `IRateProvider.getRate` is an 18-decimal fixed-point rate of one token to a related token, with no rounding argument. `ScalingHelpers.toScaled18ApplyRateRoundDown` computes `(amount * scalingFactor).mulDown(tokenRate)` and applies that one rate to the amount. For an 18-decimal share, the decimal scaling factor is 1. A rate of 1e18 means one whole share equals one whole related token in 18-decimal space. Stage one's raw rate of 1e6 is not that number. Wiring `exchangeRate()` to `getRate()` mis-scales the share. ABI compatibility does not fix it.

A normalized one-share executable quote is also not fit as that multiplier. Correct decimal normalization would turn "raw target out for 1e18 raw shares" into an 18-decimal whole-token rate. Balancer would still multiply every balance by that single rate. The hook's real exit for a large swap would differ. Arbitrage between the linear rate and the executable exit is the hole. Using the spot accounting rate as the multiplier is a different product choice, not an accepted substitute for the remembered one-share exit quote, and not what stage one authorized. I do not adopt either as the downstream invariant.

Own-pool TWAP. If the target has liquidity only in the backing pool, the conversion has to be a swap in that pool. An underlying-pool TWAP is a lagging read of that same liquidity. It is not an independent market. It can be a short-window reference. It does not make a large exit fair, and it does not make a linear rate safe. Do not add Chainlink, a peg, or a second pool to close this design. Caller minima remain the execution bound. They are not a fair-value proof.

The other four, briefly. ALPS can skip V4 conversion when its adapter returns a zero TWAP, so USDC exit is not synchronous. USDa can pay both tokens and bypass the aggregate minimum when its off-pool swap fails; that market is also outside the premise. Unoxi settles in rounds, can refuse, and its published script uses an operator-posted pin. ZapVault has no pooled ERC20, no user minimum, and can retain residual ETH. None is a base for this hook.
