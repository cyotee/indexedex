## 1. Verified corrections to peer claims

**Grok — capacity-max algorithm directionally broken.** "Compute `floor(C·1e9/P)`, then decrement while mulDiv exceeds C" (grok:19): the decrement can never trigger. For `n = floor(C·U/P)`: `n·P ≤ C·U` ⇒ `floor(n·P/U) ≤ C` always — the candidate is *always feasible*, and the true max can be **larger**. Counterexample: U=10, P=3, C=2 → candidate 6, but n=9 satisfies `floor(9·3/10)=2 ≤ 2`; true max is 9. Exact form (mine round-2, identical to Astra's `bCap`, astra:20–23): `n_max = ceil((C+1)·U/P) − 1 = ((C+1)·U − 1)/P` for positive integers (identity `ceil(a/b)−1 = floor((a−1)/b)`). For WAD-scale `P ≫ U` the gap is <1 NET-wei, so Grok's value is a safe practical underestimate — but as an algorithm it is wrong, and the closed form costs nothing. MiniMax's `n_ib_max = floor(c_WAD·1e9/P)` (minimax:7,59) is the same safe underestimate.

**Grok — tax receipt formula wrong by integer rounding.** "Pair receives `floor(nS × 9500/10000)`" (grok:47). Actual: `T(g) = g − floor(g·t/D)` (`NET.sol:136,143`). Counterexample: g=3, t=500, D=10000 → actual receipt 3 (`floor(0.15)=0` tax); Grok's form gives `floor(2.85)=2`. Astra (:25) and MiniMax (§2) have the correct form. My round-2 toy used `(1−t)` multiplicatively — acceptable only because labeled continuous-toy; production must use `g − floor(g·t/D)`.

**Grok — protocol-fee parity claim wrong.** "Do not copy QuoteTarget's `_calculateProtocolFee` + 16667; that is a different formula from canonical Uni V2" (grok:62). False: `ConstProdUtils._calculateProtocolFee` special-cases `ownerFeeShare ∈ [16666,16667]` to the canonical Uniswap branch `S·(uniRootK − uniRootKLast)/(5·uniRootK + uniRootKLast)` with `UniV2Math._sqrt` (`ConstProdUtils.sol:951–960`) — identical math to `_calculateProtocolFeeMint` (`:984–995`). QuoteTarget's `_quoteMintPoolFee` (`QuoteTarget.sol:212–223`) is therefore canonical-formula, plus QuoteState mutations for projection. MiniMax's "reuse `_quoteMintPoolFee`, do not re-derive" (§2.2, §6) stands. Residual verification, not a formula dispute: `UNISWAP_PROTOCOL_FEE_SHARE == 16667` and Astra's port-parity flag (local pair fee-off branch writes local `kLast_` vs upstream clearing storage, astra:91) remain open checks.

**Grok — router "refund" mechanism wrong.** "Router addLiquidity refunds the overweight token to msg.sender" (grok:64). Canonical Router02 `addLiquidity` trims to optimal amounts and only pulls the used amounts — no refund exists; and the SE path `_swapDeposit` (UniswapV2Service.sol:339–377) deposits balanced amounts computed in advance. In the SE, untrimmed excess would sit as vault-held balance → unbooked surplus, which under the v0.32 origin-independent pretransfer rule is claimable by the **next** caller, not the funding user. Astra's design is correct: compute minimal required deposits (`ceil(L·X'/S')`, `ceil(L·Y'/S')` + exact tax inverse), transfer only those, measure minted LP (astra:85). **Correction to my own round-2:** my "refund or unbooked pretransfer credit" residual option is unsafe for exactly this reason — prefer leftover-avoidance by construction plus direct refund of operation-attributable dust to the funding caller; unbooked credit is not a safe residual bin.

**Grok — order/direction and "dominated" claims wrong.** "Pool-first makes the following bid slightly worse" (grok:37): wrong direction. A pool swap retains the 0.3% fee, growing `k`; `polRfv = 2√(x·y)·share` grows with fees (`Treasury.sol:92–103`, whose own comment says "k-invariant under swaps, grows with fees"), so `B` and hence `price()` rise — the following bid pays **more**. Astra (:66–68) has this right, including the consequential point that a pool-first objective `(A−m)·p(m)` invalidates reusing the fixed-`p` optimum. **Correction to my own round-2:** my "pool sells do not change B; order-independent" was overstated — strictly, pool-first raises the later bid price (treasury-LP-share-weighted, tiny); order-invariance is only approximate. "Repeated bid calls are dominated" (grok:35): wrong on payouts. Later fills price higher (`(R−p·b)/(S−b) > R/S`, astra:70–76; minimax §10), so splitting a fixed NET amount across fills yields weakly **more** USDG modulo floors. One-call-per-operation is justified by gas, atomic capacity checking, and negligible drift — not dominance. MiniMax's one-call operation-scoped policy and Astra's "no unlimited micro-fill loop; one-call optimizer not proven globally best" are the correct framings.

**MiniMax — no-fee zap optimum formula wrong.** "`n_pool* = n·x/(n+2x)`… matches the standard V2 single-input zap" (minimax:17). Counterexample: x=100, n=100 → MiniMax gives 33.3; the correct no-fee solution of `(n−s)·y/(x+s)`-proportionality is `s = √(x(x+n)) − x = 41.4`. The fee-correct quadratic family is what `_swapDepositSaleAmt` implements (`ConstProdUtils.sol:457–470`). His Objective-B monotonicity discussion is otherwise sound.

**MiniMax — zap operation rule overfills the bid.** Step 4 (minimax:59): "if `r_ib_eff > r_pool_µ(0)`, set `n_ib = n_ib_max_eff`" for zap-in. For the LP objective, the bid amount is bounded by the ratio-funding quantity `b*`, not `min(capacity, n)`: beyond `b*` the extra USDG is leftover waste even if `p` still beats pool marginal (Grok:60; Astra:60 "selling all available capacity can leave too little NET to deposit"). His own §1 monotonicity text partially contradicts his operational rule; the operational rule must be corrected to the objective-constrained solver.

**MiniMax — USDG "dust ownership" product question is vacuous.** (minimax:32, options (a)/(b)/(c)). The truncation `usdgRaw = floor(payoutWad/F)` (`InverseBond.sol:82`) means the treasury simply transfers less; no dust token ever moves, so there is nothing to attribute to vault, caller, or "stuck." The only real effect is capacity accounting: `filledInEpoch += payoutWad` debits WAD including sub-unit dust (`:80`; Astra:23 "capacity debits w, not q·F" — agreement). The SE must not book phantom residual revenue.

**MiniMax — internal inconsistency + wrong atomicity rationale on fallback.** §2: "on ExceedsCapacity, revert to pool-only within the same tx"; §3 step 7: "revert the whole operation… stricter than Kimi's same-tx pool fallback"; §14: "open product decision." His rationale (b) — "unwinding pool-side accounting after IB already moved treasury reserves is non-trivial" — is mistaken twice over: a top-level revert rolls back external treasury state too, and under IB-first ordering no pool-side state exists at catch time. Reconciled position: with **IB-first**, a reverted `swap` rolls back its own burn/pull atomically, so catch-and-reroute-to-pool is state-safe (residual note: reset or exact-size the IB allowance). This **updates my round-1 cross-review preference** for revert-on-failure: both policies are sound; fallback is better UX, revert is simpler to analyze — product decision, with Grok's binding caveat: never deliver below the committed pool-floor minimum (grok:76).
