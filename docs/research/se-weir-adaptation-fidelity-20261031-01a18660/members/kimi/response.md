3. Weir issuance is liquidity-based. After pre-deposit compounding, `_deposit` computes full-range liquidity with a two-unit per-currency cushion. Genesis issues `liquidity - MIN_SHARES`, leaves 1,000 total shares unassigned, and later issuance is `floor(newLiquidity * totalShares / preDepositLiquidity)` (`WeirVaults.sol`, `_deposit`; WI-13 and WI-14). It does not use geometric-mean genesis, reserve-ratio issuance, removable-principal growth `G_i`, or an incumbent non-dilution inequality.
4. Weir excludes recorded idle from the subsequent-entry purchase-price denominator even though withdrawals later distribute idle pro rata. WI-14 and fixture F3 expressly preserve the resulting idle dilution. PairToken's `S1-ACC-07` was designed to prevent that outcome and therefore changes Weir economics rather than adapting them.
5. Weir's `_balance` sells half the estimated imbalance in the larger input leg, using nested spot-price floors. It does not always sell half of a single-token input, require an existing position, require both book reserves to be positive, cap input by book size, or enforce a tick-movement bound (`_balance`; WI-19). 6. Weir accepts actual signed swap deltas. Partial entry and exit fills are valid; a withdrawal zap can leave positive output in both currencies. WI-20 expressly prohibits a consumption-equality check and a strict single-token completion guarantee. PairToken supplies the opposing rule, and full-spread `FS-X04` narrows it further with `IncompleteConversion`.
7. Weir `_withdraw` removes proportional stored liquidity and idle after compounding, pays claimed idle into the manager, optionally swaps the unwanted positive leg, and settles actual nets. It permits a zero-output share burn when minima are zero and has no terminal rebootstrap branch (`_withdraw`; WI-17 through WI-20, AC23). The 1,000 unassigned genesis shares remain. PairToken's zero-payout rejection, true last-share exit, and rebootstrap are different economics.

Weir `_compound` writes `lastCompound`, pokes a nonempty position, takes a per-currency treasury cut, reinvests retained fees plus recorded idle with the two-unit cushion, updates idle from actual signed deltas, and never changes shares. 9. Weir charges a default 500-bps performance fee with a 1,000-bps cap and exposes owner-controlled treasury, fee, and pause methods. Full-spread instead inherits PairToken's no-performance-fee and no-operational-owner policy without a full-spread owner lock adopting that change.
10. Weir attributes fees generated by a deposit's own balancing swap through the subsequent add-liquidity caller delta to the depositor, without another performance-fee cut. Fees generated by a withdrawal's post-burn swap belong to the remaining position. WI-15, WI-23, and AC16 preserve this. PairToken's incumbent checkpoint and fee-attribution engine deliberately changes that treatment.
11. Weir supports initialized pools “hooked or not”; `createVault` checks initialization and duplication, not a zero hook address. PairToken's hookless-pool validation is an added deployment restriction, not a Standard Exchange requirement.

13. Both designs have permissionless compounding and automatic compounding around user operations, but their rules differ. The full-spread lock keeps the function; it does not authorize PairToken's ratio-swap, dust, cap, checkpoint, or withdrawal-fallback algorithm.
14. Weir has no conditional collection-only withdrawal. A failed pre-withdrawal compound prevents that withdrawal. Dropping `previewWithdrawalMode` under `FS-OWN-10` did not authorize retaining the internal PairToken branch.
15. Weir's `previewRedeem` is a current stored-principal-plus-idle view; it excludes pending fees and does not compound or zap. `pendingFees` is separate and gross of the performance fee (WI-25). PairToken's execution-equivalent projection, fixed 64/12/16 work bounds, dual-candidate planning, `EconomicStateChanged`, and 12M/5M limits are a separate engine, not a consequence of merely exposing accepted Standard Exchange preview selectors.

## Conclusions

### Overall conclusion

The current PRD is **not a WeirVaults adaptation with a tokenized exchange shell**. It is PairToken stage-one economics and planning with a full-spread name, while expressly disclaiming Weir. The one-position/full-range shape, actual-delta settlement, public compound concept, and some native plumbing are shared or similar, but the core ownership and execution rules are not Weir's.

The Goal, authority table, blanket S1 inheritance, `FS-MONEY-01`, and acceptance rows that require those inherited economics must lose their PairToken basis. The accepted tokenized surface can remain, but its implementation must be respecified around Weir's liquidity/share ledger, actual signed deltas, fee harvest, idle accounting, and operation ordering. No cap values or replacement PRD text are proposed here.

### Explicit authorization cases for PairToken mechanisms recommended for retention

Only the following two open mechanisms merit a recommendation beyond the locks and accepted surface.

#### Case 1: operation-scoped cross-facet write guard and expected-callback commitment

- **Weir behavior replaced or changed:** standalone Weir places nonReentrant on deposit, withdraw, and compound, while `unlockCallback` checks only `onlyPoolManager`. It does not commit an expected callback payload or share a diamond-wide guard across facets.
- **Recommended scope:** one operation-scoped write guard covering money, locked donation/compound, and callback execution; one committed operation/payload before an ordinary vault-started unlock; consume it once and reject unsolicited or replayed callbacks. This does not include economic read reversion, basic-reserve snapshots, or PairToken's per-action planner.
- **Caller or vault harm if absent:** the adapted proxy combines mutable ERC-20 shares, registry state, reserve views, native wrapping, pretransfer credit, and several externally callable facets around token/hook/manager callbacks. A callback that reenters through a different facet can bypass a guard local to one function and observe or mutate a partially settled composite operation. A manager-originated callback without an expected payload also lacks a vault-level check that it belongs to the operation currently being executed.
- **Why the owner should authorize the mix:** this is a minimal Diamond/integration control, not a change to share price, fee ownership, zap size, partial fills, or withdrawal outcomes. It preserves Weir's one-operation ordinary unlock while protecting the additional tokenized surface. The narrower standalone behavior can remain for views and non-money reads.

#### Case 2: native sync isolation on the borrowed-unlock path only

- **Weir behavior replaced or changed:** Weir pays native debt with payable `settle` and does not call native `sync`; AC51 preserves the resulting stale-ERC20-sync failure. PairToken changes every native debt to sync native first.
- **Recommended scope:** within the newly authorized borrowed-unlock `exchangeIn`, synchronize native immediately before native value settlement as part of the per-address delta-isolation sequence. Do not silently rewrite Weir's ordinary locked-path native behavior or clear another actor's ERC-20 sync.
- **Caller or vault harm if absent:** the vault is now deliberately executing inside an outer router/hook unlock whose sync checkpoint and open deltas it does not own. A still-synced ERC-20 can make native value settlement revert; attempting to work around that by clearing or reusing foreign sync state can misattribute the outer actor's credit. Either outcome breaks accepted native buffer/unwrap calls or risks settling against another participant's funds.
- **Why the owner should authorize the mix:** the harm arises from the new composition context, not from a desire to import PairToken economics. A path-local native sync is narrowly targeted at atomic per-address settlement and leaves issuance, fees, liquidity, partial fills, and the ordinary Weir path unchanged. If the owner wants the global native-sync repair too, that should be a separate explicit Weir behavior deviation.

No other open PairToken mechanism has a sufficient retention case. In particular, neither PairToken's prior presence nor generic claims of safety justify its caps, strict conversion, no-share remainder, NAV/non-dilution issuance, fee removal, dead-share removal, compound swap, withdrawal fallback, or projection budgets.

## Limits

- This is a static source/requirements assessment. I ran no build, test, deployment, RPC call, or implementation and make no runtime-equivalence or safety claim.
- I did not read peer responses or treat PairToken as product authority. The PairToken stage-one document was used only to identify inherited mechanisms.
- I rely on the full-spread documents' summaries of the accepted Standard Exchange report and owner locks; this response does not reopen those decisions.
- The PRD does not yet specify how an accepted single-output route should represent Weir's valid partial-zap second-currency output. That is an interface/behavior conflict requiring an explicit product decision, not permission to impose full conversion silently.
- The locked local sleeve has unspecified refill, shortfall, and locked-caller spending rules. Those rules must be designed against Weir idle and the locked reserve view, not inherited from PairToken.
- The interaction between the compound lock's wording and Weir's treasury cut needs owner clarification only if the lock was intended to mean that all collected fees must be reinvested. Otherwise Weir's fee behavior is the baseline.
- This response recommends requirement dispositions only. It neither replaces the PRD nor authorizes implementation.
