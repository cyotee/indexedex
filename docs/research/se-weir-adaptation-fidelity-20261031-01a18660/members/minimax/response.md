In `WeirVaults.deposit` the unused `amount0 - used0` / `amount1 - used1` is sent back to `msg.sender`. PRD FS-X04 (line 30) instead says "Addition remainder `J_i` stays booked to shared backing without extra shares, and previews disclose it," and the §2.1 dispositions table (line 98) records "Inherited `J_i` booking plus FS-X04 full selected-swap consumption."

PRD FS-X04 (line 30) requires that the selected swap "consumes its entire specified input or the operation reverts `IncompleteConversion(uint256 expectedInput, uint256 consumedInput)`."

**E6 — Weir has no zap cap of any kind.** `WeirVaults._balance` (`[W:365-388]`) computes the token-0 value at the pre-swap spot price and swaps **half** the excess, using the post-swap price for sizing. `WeirVaults._swap` (`[W:302-315]`) sets `sqrtPriceLimitX96` to `MIN_SQRT_PRICE + 1` / `MAX_SQRT_PRICE - 1`, i.e. no price-movement bound at all. The PRD nevertheless puts `uint16 maxZapBps` and `int24 maxZapTickMove` into `PkgArgs` (lines 186-187) and requires `InvalidZapLimits` for a bad bound (line 202).

**E7 — Weir compounds before every deposit and withdrawal; the PRD inherits a PairToken withdrawal-only fallback.** Import PRD WI-11 and WI-23 (`[W:175-177]`, `[W:224-235]`, `[W:263-274]`): "Deposit/withdrawal callback handling SHALL compound first, then execute the requested action"; the deposit's pre-compound event precedes its deposit event, and the withdrawal's precedes its withdrawal event. **E9 — `compound` matches Weir's semantics.** Import PRD WI-21, WI-22, WI-23 (`[W:319-359]`): compound writes the timestamp, pokes the position for fees, takes the treasury cut, reinvests net fees plus idle, updates idle, and leaves the share supply unchanged. PRD FS-OWN-09 keeps `compound()`; the function-review note says "It collects fees and puts them back into the position. It does not change the share supply." That is Weir's rule.

### C3 — Authorization cases for the keeps, and the cases the owner still needs

**Keep: `compound()` — no authorization case required, because it is Weir's own function.** Weir behavior preserved: permissionless harvest-and-reinvest on demand and before every deposit and withdrawal, with the share supply unchanged. Harm if absent: the position's fee growth never realizes into liquidity, the vault stops tracking pool fee income, and the property stated in Weir's own contract docstring — that "a share only ever grows in liquidity" — is lost, along with the only route that converts fee growth into shares' backing. Why the owner should allow it: this is fidelity, not mixing. The only reason it needs a line in the PRD is that its fee leg differs from Weir's (C4, G-b).

**Owner decision required: `donate`.** Case, if the owner wants to keep it: *Weir behavior it replaces or changes* — WI-24's rule that receipts arriving without a route stay unassigned physical inventory and are never booked, which is what keeps a stray transfer from silently changing the vault's accounting. *Harm if absent* — under the PRD's own `reserves()` definition (FS-OWN-07: local balances plus deployed amounts) a raw transfer would silently raise the reported reserves with no share issued and no preview change, which is an accounting divergence a deliberate route would close; and a third party who wants to add backing to a vault whose only routes are issuance and redemption has no route at all. *Why the owner might allow the mix* — it makes the donation intentional and disclosed rather than an accounting artifact. *Cost of keeping it as currently specified* — it creates a booked backing class that no Weir rule covers: idle is a compound-time, denominator-excluded, proportionally claimable quantity, and unassigned receipts are neither; the PRD defines no redemption or preview treatment for the donated balance, so it would sit in `reserves()` and be undistributed. If the owner keeps `donate`, the PRD must state which of those two Weir classes it is. My recommendation is to drop it from the Weir-based family, and to leave the raw-transfer semantics of WI-24 as the rule.

**Owner decision required: the zap price-impact guard.** The risk PairToken's `maxZapTickMove` was introduced for is real and is not a PairToken artifact: Weir's `_swap` sets the price limit to the sqrt extremes, so a single depositor can move the pool price arbitrarily far in one transaction, and because issuance is priced from the post-zap state, that movement is externalized onto existing holders and onto every quote the standard produces. *Weir behavior a guard would change* — the unbounded `sqrtPriceLimitX96` in `_swap` and the half-excess balancing in `_balance`. *Harm if absent* — a large single-token entry can move the share price an arbitrary distance inside one transaction, which in a tokenized, continuously quoted share is observable loss to holders rather than a private rounding effect. *Why the owner might allow the mix* — the tokenized surface quotes this vault continuously and the loss is externalized. *Preferred form if authorized* — the Weier-shaped guard is to bound the zap swap's own `sqrtPriceLimitX96`, and, if a stored bound is wanted, to revert when the traversed tick range exceeds it; that avoids importing PairToken's `PkgArgs` field, its validation rule, and `InvalidZapLimits`. No number is proposed here, and I am not recommending a value.

**Owner decision required, only if the owner wants the PairToken rule: keeping the `J_i` booking.** *Weir behavior it replaces* — "unused declared input is refunded to the depositor and never becomes vault backing" (WI-12, WI-15). *Harm if absent* — none to the depositor, because the accepted standard already returns `amountRefunded` on the same route (FS-OWN-08); the only loss is that the vault stops silently absorbing the depositor's unspent wei. *Why the owner might still authorize it* — if a single-token entry is meant to contribute to shared backing rather than be returned, then the remainder must be excluded from the issuance denominator (Weir's WI-14 rule for idle) and given a stated preview and redemption treatment, or it dilutes existing holders. Without those two rules the booking is strictly worse than the refund.

### C4 — The Weir behaviors the PRD does not carry (fidelity gaps, not carry-overs)

These are the other half of the owner's question: the PRD is not only carrying PairToken economics, it is also missing parts of Weir. Each needs a decision; none is answered by a lock.

- **G-a — Genesis issuance floor.** Weir WI-13: at first issuance `l <= 1_000` reverts `ZeroShares()`; otherwise `l - 1_000` shares are issued and 1,000 shares stay assigned to no address. The PRD's "no permanent dead-share mint" (line 208) removes both halves, and the anti-inflation half was the point: the reserve stops the first deposit from setting an arbitrary share price. The revert-on-tiny-first-deposit rule is not a dead-share mint and can be kept on its own as a first-deposit minimum.
- **G-b — Treasury performance fee.** Weir takes 500 bps of harvested fees by default, capped at 1,000 bps, to a treasury address (WI-22; `[W:34-36]`, `[W:79]`). The PRD drops it (line 208), so 100% of fees would compound into the position. This is defensible because the standard has its own fee surface (`vaultFeeTypeIds`, `vaultConfig`, S1-ACC-06 attribution), but it must be recorded as a deliberate divergence, with its consequences stated: there is no treasury, `setTreasury`/`setPerfFee` do not exist, and the compound inventory formula changes.
- **G-c — Pre-action compounding order.** Weir compounds, then acts, on every deposit and every withdrawal, and the withdrawal sizes against the post-compound state (WI-11, WI-17, WI-23; E7). The PRD inherits a PairToken withdrawal-only "collection-only fallback" instead and says deposits do not compound (line 220, FS-A11 line 354). A collection-only mode does not exist in Weir at all: `_compound` always harvests, cuts, reinvests, and updates idle.
- **G-d — Idle accounting.** Weir's `idle0`/`idle1` have a formula (`R_j = F_j - C_j + I_j`, two-unit cushion, residual becomes idle), are excluded from the issuance denominator, and are claimed proportionally at withdrawal (WI-14, WI-18, WI-22). The PRD mentions "the idle currencies of that position" in the goal (line 9) and defines idle nowhere, while `J_i` occupies the same conceptual slot under different rules. Idle and `J_i` must not both exist.
- **G-e — Hooked-pool support.** Weir passes caller-supplied `hookData` unchanged into every `modifyLiquidity` and `swap`, which is what lets it run on hooked pools (WI-11; E11). The PRD never mentions `hookData`. If the standard's route signatures carry none, the adaptation silently drops hooked-pool support for a vault whose whole hook-integration story (FS-HOOK-01/02) assumes the hook can drive it.
- **G-f — Pause.** Weir pauses deposits only; withdrawals, compound, transfers and views keep working (WI-26). The PRD never mentions pause. If the standard supplies no pause, the family loses an operational control Weir has.
- **G-g — Single-leg exit.** Weir's exit can zap out to one currency (`zapTo` 1 or 2, WI-19). FS-OWN-09's mapped redeem "removes both currencies proportionally. It does not sell one leg to pay the other" (line 62), which removes that Weir behavior, while FS-A08 asserts a single-token exit exists (line 351) and the §6 table calls `previewExchangeIn` an exit (line 260). The PRD is internally inconsistent here (E14), and the resolution matters: under Weir the exit zap is what converts the exiting holder's share of the unwanted leg, and the mapped route's stated rule removes it.
- **G-h — The balancing heuristic.** The PRD never states how the single-token entry decides its swap size (E15). Weir's rule is exact — value token 0 at the pre-swap spot price, swap half the difference, size at the post-swap price — and is not interchangeable with a "swap the whole input" rule. Leaving it unspecified lets the implementation invent it.
- **G-i — Views.** Weir's `previewRedeem` and `pendingFees` have no counterpart in the PRD (E11). Their replacements are the standard's accepted exit quote and `reserves()`. The PRD should say so, so a later reviewer does not read their absence as a gap and re-add them.

### C5 — Not carry-overs; leave them alone

These are required by the tokenized surface or by an owner lock, and the brief is right that they should not be re-litigated: the borrowed-unlock path and `InvalidUnlockedContext` (required by the buffer-hook surface — Weir has no borrowed unlock, but the standard's `exchangeIn` is called inside a hook's unlock); `ExchangeOutNotAvailable()` and the closed exact-output surface; the `(amountOut, amountRefunded)` return, which is Weir-consistent (E12); the twelve-field `PkgInit` and the five shared facets, which are SE package law rather than a Weir-or-PairToken choice — though `PkgArgs` is not package law and is addressed in C2; `reserves()`, `reserveOfToken`, `localReserves()` and the liquidity sleeve (FS-OWN-04/07) — noting the sleeve must not become a second issuance denominator, which is Weir's WI-14 rule; the Position Repo, stored ticks, `positionLiquidity()` and the Protocol Aware pair (FS-OWN-01/02/06); and the gas ceilings, which are repo test law.

### C6 — What I would change in the PRD, in one list
