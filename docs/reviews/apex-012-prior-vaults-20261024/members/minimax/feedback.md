# Disposition

**Support-with-reservations.**

The draft's central narrowing is mechanically correct and corrects my own response. My `response.md` grouped "prices from live pool depth or live `slot0`" with "does not stop this claim", which conflates a spot mark with depth sensitivity. Only Wings' simulated sale into remaining depth makes *added outside depth* an input to the amount paid; a share of the vault's own position (Arrakis V2 `S4`, Charm `S14`) and a spot mark (Gamma V4 `S13`) are not depth-sensitive at fixed spot, because their recorded formulas use the vault's own liquidity and a price, and adding liquidity does not move a price. The draft states my position accurately ("MiniMax and Kimi group the slot0 readers with designs that do not stop the claim"), so there is no misstatement of my reading to correct there — that attribution is fair and should not be softened.

My reservations are about framing, completeness, and evidence attribution in the final report, plus one error that exists in my own `response.md` and that the draft happens to avoid: the cached-mark value is recorded as *not* reading the pool, so my claim that "a later poke reflects the added depth" is unsupported by the admitted sources and must not propagate into the report. That correction is recorded here because I was instructed not to edit `response.md`.

I do not propose a vault redesign; the points below concern how evidence is stated, not what IndexedEx should build.

# Concerns

**C1 — Correct my own over-broad grouping (concession, not a defect).** My `response.md` Q1/Q2 lumped Arrakis V2 (`S4`), Charm (`S14`) and Gamma V4 (`S13`) with Wings as designs that "do not stop this claim", on the basis that they read live `slot0`. That basis is wrong for the liquidity channel. The draft's narrower reading should stand in the final report, and the report should say so in one clause, because a reader comparing the brief's question with the answer would otherwise see an unexplained change of position.

**C2 — My `response.md` contains an error the draft avoids; correct it in the report's inputs.** In `response.md` (Q2 and Limits) I wrote that the `GUniLPOracle` cached value "derives from live pool liquidity and price at poke time, so a later poke reflects the added depth". The admitted sources do not support this. SYNTHESIS "Alternative precedent, not the recommendation" records that the precedent "derives `sqrtPriceX96` from two independent USD oracles, calls `getUnderlyingBalancesAtPrice`, and divides by supply" and that "that cached value is a view a rate provider can call **without reading the pool** in the caller's transaction". On the survey's own reading, a pure depth change with an unchanged price does not move the cached value at all, even at a fresh poke. The residual uncertainty is different from what I wrote: the survey does not record who may call `poke()`, and `seek()` "still calls the vault". The draft's phrasing ("A liquidity change in the attacker's own transaction does not rewrite that cached number. A later poke can.") is therefore slightly stronger than the sources allow on the "a later poke can" clause, and softer on the depth-sensitivity point, where it happens to be right for a different reason than I gave. Recommendation: rewrite that sentence to the recorded mechanism — the mark does not read the pool, its price input is external, so it is not depth-sensitive; the open questions are `poke()` permission and whether the withdrawal path actually pays the cached number.

**C3 — "What can be taken" overstates three pieces.** The section reads as three candidate routes for IndexedEx. Each needs a qualifier the survey already supplies:

- **Arrakis V2 is deprecated on 15 Jun 2026** (`S24`; SYNTHESIS "Recommendation" exclusion line). A deprecated protocol is a witness to a formula, not a takeable piece. Keep the formula as negative evidence and say the protocol itself is retired.
- **Charm repeats the one-token-exit cost IndexedEx already bears.** The draft names "the one-token exit" as the cost but does not say that this is the same product requirement the vault exists to meet, so the cost is not a price the product can pay for a liquidity fix. It also does not stop the spot-move sequence, which the draft says in passing but should connect to the claim report's separate spot sequence (see C9).
- **The cached mark is labelled by the survey as a precedent, not a candidate.** SYNTHESIS "Alternative precedent, not the recommendation" says verbatim: "it is not the recommended pattern". The draft keeps "does not make a live at-price call safe" but drops the "not the recommended pattern" label while placing the item under "what can be taken". Add the label, or retitle the section so that none of the three reads as a selected direction. Suggested title: "What the survey supports as candidates (none is a finished vault, none is a decision)".

**C4 — Two sentences rewrite the survey's predicate, which is an evidence-fidelity problem in a section whose whole point is that the survey did not test this claim.**

- "No inspected production Uniswap vault was shown to earn swap fees and pay a one-token amount that ignores a permitted liquidity change." The survey's finding is about a rate that "ignores the same pool's **spot price**" (SYNTHESIS "Executive summary"). The draft's sentence substitutes the new question's predicate for the old question's, then corrects itself in the next line ("The survey did not test any design against APEX-2026-012"). Written as one sentence in a summary, the substituted version will be quoted without the correction. Recommend keeping the survey's own predicate and stating the liquidity property as untested.
- "Wings is the only inspected design recorded as pricing a withdrawal from other liquidity providers' live depth." The survey records `_quote simulates opposing-token sale` (`S3`); that the simulated sale lands in *other providers'* depth, and that only the vault's own liquidity is subtracted, comes from the claim report and the optional-context section ("`_removeOwned` ... subtracts only this vault's own L"), not from the survey. Attribute each half to its source.

**C5 — Panoptic precision and labelling consistency.** The draft says "`convertToAssets` reads stored assets and supply". The recorded formula is `convertToAssets` using stored `s_poolAssets + s_inAMM` (`S16`); the division by supply is part of the synthesis's *recommended pattern*, and the Panoptic row's verdict is "Shape only. Not an LP vault." Also, the draft labels the Arrakis/Charm inference ("This is an inference from the recorded formulas, not a test, and not a synthesis finding") but presents the Panoptic inference ("An outsider adding Uniswap liquidity does not write those stores, so that formula would not pay more because depth changed") without the same label. Apply the same label to both, and quote the stored fields as recorded.

**C6 — The stored-counter cost is compressed; the retention/loss split matters.** The draft says "The cost is that fees and the other token stay out of the amount until a real credit writes the counter." That is accurate but hides the split I recorded, which is the operationally important part: **fee earning is not given up** — SYNTHESIS "Recommendation" says "the Uniswap position still earns swap fees while price is inside the range" — while **continuous fee recognition in the rate is**: fees "enter the rate only when target tokens actually move into the accounted balance: a settled swap, a harvest, a deposit, or a redemption", so the rate is flat between conversions. For a product whose promise is a compounding one-token rate, that is a behavioural change and should be stated as its own cost rather than folded into "fees stay out of the amount".

**C7 — The cached-mark item needs three additions.** (a) The survey's own label, per C3. (b) The survey does not record who may call `poke()`, so the staleness bound is "the last poke, at most one hop old by default" and the refresh question is open. (c) Under the draft's narrowing, the recorded `slot0` read in Arrakis V2 `underlying()` for the **fee tick** is a tick read, not a sale into depth, so it does not make added depth an input to the paid amount. The draft juxtaposes "Arrakis still reads slot0 for the fee tick" with the narrowing without that reasoning, and a reader may read the juxtaposition as "still depth-sensitive". State the reasoning in one clause.

**C8 — Three survey records are absent from the draft's map.**

- **Steer** (`S15`): the one inspected design where the survey records that the *integrator* supplies the price ("Pushes the price choice to the caller"). Under the draft's own narrowing it is depth-neutral at fixed spot and a fixed external price, which makes it the most decision-relevant omission: the survey hands the price choice to IndexedEx. One line saying it contributes nothing here would close the gap.
- **Revert `V3Oracle`** (`S17`): `twapSeconds == 0` uses `slot0`, else `pool.observe`. Under the narrowing, the `slot0` branch is a spot mark, not depth-sensitivity; recorded so the report does not leave a live-spot branch unmentioned.
- **Balancer `ERC4626RateProvider`** (`S21`): "lane web-balancer-rates, not re-read by lead", and `getRate` delegates to `convertToAssets`. It is an unverified external rate-provider precedent. If it stays out of the report, say it stayed out for that reason; if it goes in, it needs a verification pass the survey did not perform.

**C9 — The claim's other two broadenings are dropped.** The claim report states the report is broader in two places than the clarification: it also claims the spot-move sequence (swap, deposit, swap back to the exact original price, redeem, paying 0.3% fees) and the unsettled in-unlock path. The draft's own cost line concedes that Charm "does not stop the spot-move sequence", which means the spot channel is live and open — but the draft never says the auditor claims it. Since the final report will be read as the council's statement of what APEX-2026-012 covers, one line should record that the narrowing addressed the liquidity channel only and that the spot-move sequence is a separate, unaddressed channel (and that the in-unlock path is blocked by a scripted 0% sleeve while the settled path is not, per the claim report).

**C10 — The brief's Q1 premise is the thing being corrected, and the report should say so.** The brief asked which designs "price a withdrawal from live pool depth or live `slot0`, **and therefore** do not stop this claim". The draft shows that the "and therefore" is invalid: reading `slot0` is not depth-sensitivity. One sentence in the final report — that the scoping question conflated a spot mark with depth sensitivity, and the survey's answers were re-read accordingly — makes the narrowed list legible instead of looking like a thinner answer to the same question.

**C11 — "Excluded from the recommendation set" is not a security clearance of the Wings mechanism.** SYNTHESIS "Recommendation" excluded "Wings spot liquidation" as a *rate source* and the table row for Wings reads "No. This is the bug." That exclusion was made under the old spot-rate question, and the survey's exclusion criterion was spot-only throughout: a design can pass the survey's spot test and still read live depth. The final report should say that the exclusion list carries no clearance for the depth channel, so nobody later reads "excluded" as "safe". This is also the general form of the point the draft already makes for the recommendation ("The survey did not test any design against APEX-2026-012") and belongs next to it.
